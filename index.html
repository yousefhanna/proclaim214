Ontario is late.

We passed the law to stop changing the clocks in 2020. It received royal assent. It has never been switched on. Six provinces and territories stop on 1 November — Ontario doesn't, and it needs one signature to join them.

The switching is the part that hurts

Not the hour we keep — the act of moving it. Every March and every November, the same damage turns up in the same figures.

+6%Fatal collisions in the week after we spring forward
+11%Diagnosed depressive episodes after we fall back, fading over ten weeks
MondayCardiac admissions and workplace injuries both spike on the first one

No single one of these is a crisis. Together they are a cost the province chooses to pay, twice a year, on dates it sets itself. Queen's Park read this evidence in 2020 and voted unanimously to stop paying it.

A law nobody voted against, waiting on a vote in Albany

The Time Amendment Act, 2020 comes into force when the Attorney General proclaims it. The government said it would do that once Quebec and New York State did the same.

New York can't. No American state may adopt permanent daylight time without an act of Congress, and New York's own bill is written to wait on Connecticut, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Pennsylvania and Vermont first. The federal Sunshine Protection Act passed the House 308–117 in July 2026 and now sits in the Senate, where the identical idea passed in 2022 and then died.

So a decision that belongs to Ontario was handed to a foreign legislature, which handed it on to five more, which are waiting on a Congress that hasn't finished in eight years of trying. That isn't coordination. It's an indefinite delay with a polite name.

We spent this year arguing the opposite

In November 2025 every province and territory signed the Canadian Mutual Recognition Agreement: a good lawfully sold in one jurisdiction can be sold in all of them. The IMF puts the value of finishing that work at roughly $210 billion in real GDP.

When the federal minister responsible explained what mutual recognition was for, the example she reached for was a trucker who shouldn't have to move his lights to a slightly different position because he crossed a provincial boundary.

We agreed truckers shouldn't move their headlights. We still make them move their clocks.

Ontario didn't just sign that agreement — Ontario led it. In July the Canadian Federation of Independent Business gave the province an A+, a grade outside its own published scale, for holding no exceptions whatsoever under the Canadian Free Trade Agreement. Ontario's own Protect Ontario Through Free Trade Within Canada Act put an "as of right" framework in place on 1 January, spanning more than fifty regulators and some three hundred certifications.

One arbitrary difference between Canadian jurisdictions is left that Ontario could remove by signature. On that one, we are harmonizing with New York State instead of with Manitoba.

Doing nothing has a price now, too. From November, Manitoba sits at UTC−5 all year — Ontario's winter clock. So the gap between the two provinces will be an hour in summer and nothing in winter, where for decades it was simply an hour. Any dispatcher scheduling across that border now has to track which half of the year it is. That is a new barrier between two Canadian provinces, put up in the same year Ontario was graded top of the class for pulling them down, and we built it by sitting still.

The arguments against

Sleep scientists say permanent standard time.

They do, and it's the strongest objection to us. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine, the Canadian Sleep Society and the Canadian Society for Chronobiology all favour standard time on circadian grounds. We think they're right that morning light matters and wrong about the priority. The switching is what the harm is measured from; Ontario's law provides for daylight time, and Quebec's and Manitoba's consultations both asked for it. Stop changing first. Argue about which hour to keep after.

Winter mornings will be dark.

True, and it's the real cost. Toronto sunrise reaches roughly 8:50 a.m. in late December. School boards can shift start times, and several Canadian jurisdictions are about to demonstrate how that works in practice. This is the trade-off the legislature already weighed and accepted in 2020.

Polling says people prefer standard time.

American polling does. Canadian consultations keep landing the other way — Manitoba 58% to 34% for daylight time, Quebec roughly three-quarters. Ask Canadians and you get a different answer.

We'll be out of step with the United States.

We're already out of step with six Canadian jurisdictions, and from November we'll be out of step with Manitoba in two directions depending on the season. The question was never whether to align. It's who with.

Where these numbers come from

  1. Bill 214, Time Amendment Act, 2020 — royal assent 30 November 2020; in force by proclamation, conditioned on Quebec and New York State.
  2. Province of Manitoba, 17 September 2026 — permanent daylight time; consultation ~72,000 responses, 58% daylight / 34% standard.
  3. Province of British Columbia — final clock change 8 March 2026; 2019 engagement, 223,000 responses, 93% in favour of ending changes.
  4. Alberta Bill 31, tabled 23 April 2026 — permanent Mountain Daylight Time from the November transition.
  5. Gouvernement du Québec consultation, 2024–25 — 214,000+ responses; 91% to abolish, roughly three-quarters preferring daylight time.
  6. H.R. 139, Sunshine Protection Act — passed the U.S. House 308–117 on 14 July 2026; pending in the Senate.
  7. Canadian Mutual Recognition Agreement — signed 19 November 2025; in force June 2026. IMF estimate of ~$210 billion via CFIB.
  8. CFIB, State of Internal Trade, July 2026 — Ontario and the federal government awarded A+ on Canadian Free Trade Agreement exceptions for holding none.
  9. Fritz et al., Current Biology (2020) — ~6% rise in fatal crashes in the week after the spring transition.
  10. Hansen et al., Epidemiology (2017) — 11% rise in incidence of unipolar depressive episodes after the autumn transition, across 185,419 hospital contacts.
  11. MPP contact details read from each member's page at ola.org. 124 seats, 3 currently vacant. The Attorney General may also be reached through the Ministry of the Attorney General.
